Let's calculate the expected number of tail items/batch and its variance
in order to understand the expected variance
note that for each positive, there are batch_size - 1 negatives 
in the in-batch softmax loss.

# train ratings intersect             =   454_245
# train ratings intersect, tail(tier=2) =   4_291
p_tail = 4_291/454_245 = 0.00924

batch_size      λ as expected tails   
  256           2.4
 1024           9.7
 2048          19.3 

Sampling in batches follows a binomial distribution
(technically, we are drawing without replacement which is
hypergeometric distribution, but with large N that is equiv
to the bionmial distribution).

for small p such as p_tail 0.009 we can use Poisson distribution.
mean is λ 
variance is λ, hence st dev = sqrt(λ) 

The coefficient of variation is the ratio of the st dev to the mean:
    CV = sqrt(λ) / λ = 1/sqrt(λ)

batch_size      λ as expected tails   stdev(=S/N)   CV         comment
  256           2.4                   1.56         0.64        low signal-to-noise (S/N)
 1024           9.7                   3.11         0.32        
 1059          10.0                   3.16         0.31        S/N here and higher for gaussian
 2048          19.3                   4.40         0.23        

    at λ >= 10 the poisson distribution resembles a gaussian distribution

    a 95% confidence interval is λ += 2 * sqrt(λ)

For stable in-batch softmax learning—especially when using heavily 
weighted loss functions like focal loss or IPW — a standard 
statistical rule of thumb is that the minority class must have 
an expected representation of λ >= 10 per batch and then the
long tail where k=0 vanishes.  (see k=0 calcs below).

Let's calculate the likelihood of counting 0 or less than some
number to be determined number of tails in a batch.

P(k) = (exp^(-λ)) * (λ^(k))/ k!
    where k is the number of tail items in a batch.
 
batch_size    P(0)       P(1)    
   256        8.9E-02   2.1E-01 
  1024        6.3E-05   6.1E-04 
  2048        4.0E-09   7.7E-08 

for the batch_size=256, 1 out of 11 times the model will see 0 tail items.
                        1 out of 5 times the model will see 1 tail item
    - 95% of all batches will contain between  (λ ± 2√λ)
        = 2.4 += 2*1.56 = 0 to 5 tail items
   - for the 1 out of 11 times where the batch positive is not a tail item,
     the learning is purely torso and head
   - for the 1 out of 5 times when the positive is a tail, it is not very 
     likely that there will be a tail item in the 254 negatives and so
     learning tail-to-tail relationships is nearly impossible in this case.
   - for the case when there is a positive tail item, the IPW weights
       are increased leading to very large gradient, in contrast to
       the 0s before, leading to erratic and large variations (unstable)
    => expect unstable learning of the tail distribution

for the batch_size=1024, 1 out of 15_885 times the model will see 0 tail items.
                         1 out of 1_642 times the model will see 1 tail item
    - 95% of all batches will contain between  (λ ± 2√λ)
        = 10 += 6.28 = 3 to 16 tail items
    - for case of 1 positive tail and 3 to 16 negatives that are tail items
      the model successfully learns negative boundaries for the tail content
    - the IPW multipliers are no longer rarely increasing tail weights,
      smoothing out the gradient updates and reducing variation.
    => expect stable learning of the tail distribution
   
or the batch_size=2048, 1 out of 252 million times the model will see 0 tail items.
                        1 out of 13 million times the model will see 1 tail item
    - 95% of all batches will contain between  (λ ± 2√λ)
        = 19.3 += 2*4.4 = 10 to 28 tail items
    - for case of 1 positive tail and 10 to 28 negatives that are tail items
      the model successfully learns negative boundaries for the tail content
    - the IPW multipliers are no longer rarely increasing tail weights,
      smoothing out the gradient updates and reducing variation.
    => expect highly stable learning of the tail distribution
